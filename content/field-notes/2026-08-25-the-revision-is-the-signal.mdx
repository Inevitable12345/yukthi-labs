In May 2021, AlixPartners estimated that semiconductor shortages would cost vehicle manufacturers
USD 110 billion and 3.9 million units of production. By September the same analysts had revised the
figure to USD 210 billion and 7.7 million units. <EvidenceMarker id="E-007" />

The number that gets quoted is the second one. The more informative object is the ratio between them.

## What a doubling means

A forecast that nearly doubles within four months is not usually a forecast that was badly executed.
It is a forecast whose underlying structure was incomplete — pathways were active that the model
did not contain, so each new one arrived as a surprise rather than as an update to a known term.

That is a structural diagnosis, and it is available _at the time of the revision_, without
hindsight. The revision itself is the signal.

## The same shape elsewhere

The ECB's review of its own 2021–22 inflation projections describes the same pattern in a very
different domain: accuracy deteriorating sharply, with errors concentrated where energy dynamics
and supply bottlenecks had changed what generated the data. <EvidenceMarker id="E-008" />

Two well-resourced forecasting efforts, in unrelated fields, failing in the same characteristic
way at roughly the same time. That co-occurrence is itself weak evidence for a common cause —
a change in structure rather than a run of bad luck in two places.

## A monitoring proposal

Treat large mid-horizon revisions as a first-class observable.

1. Record the revision magnitude, not just the current estimate.
2. Where a revision exceeds some threshold, flag the _model_ rather than the outlook.
3. Ask which pathway was missing, and whether it is now represented.

None of that requires a better forecast. It requires noticing that the forecast moved, and treating
the movement as a fact about the representation.
