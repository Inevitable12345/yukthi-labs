The joint FERC/NERC inquiry into February 2021 documents roughly 61,800 MW of generation lost,
1,045 units experiencing 4,124 outages, derates or failures to start, and more than 4.5 million
customers without power. <EvidenceMarker id="E-010" />

Those are the numbers. The mechanism is more interesting than any of them.

## The edge

Natural gas production, gathering, compression and processing draw electricity from the grid that
gas-fired generation supplies. Shedding load to protect the grid therefore reduced the fuel
available to restore it.

For the duration of the event, electricity and gas were not two coupled sectors. They were one
system, and the coupling term had a positive sign.

## Why nobody modelled it

Because it is nobody's model. An excellent model of the power system and an excellent model of the
gas system, held by different organisations with different regulators and different data, cannot
between them represent the edge that connects them. The edge falls in the gap.

This generalises uncomfortably well. The highest-consequence relations in a coupled system tend to
be precisely the ones that cross an institutional boundary, because a boundary is where
responsibility for modelling stops.

## Where the same shape is forming now

Data-centre electricity demand is projected to roughly double to about 945 TWh by 2030 on the IEA's
base case. <EvidenceMarker id="E-015" /> That load arrives through transformers and grid connection
equipment, which arrive through material supply chains, which pass through concentrated processing.

Compute capacity, grid hardware and critical-mineral policy are currently modelled by three
communities that rarely share a diagram. On the evidence of 2021, that is the interesting place
to look.
